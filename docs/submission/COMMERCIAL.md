# Commercial Positioning

## Positioning statement
FluxMandate is the control layer between signed commercial agreements and autonomous payment software. It does not replace ERPs, banks, treasury or accounting systems; it adds the one thing they lack: machine-enforceable, contract-linked spending authority with an audit trail.

## Customer problem (in their words)
- "We let an agent use the company card and hope."
- "Every invoice still needs a human because we can't set a limit that matches the contract."
- "Audit asks why the agent paid this and we can't show the clause."

## Initial customer (beachhead hypothesis)
Small-to-mid technology companies and crypto-native organizations (startups, DAOs, treasuries) whose AI agents already pay for compute, data, APIs or SaaS under vendor contracts. They have the problem now, tolerate new tooling, and often already hold stablecoins. **Hypothesis, not validated.**

## Value by buyer
| Buyer | Value |
|---|---|
| CFO / finance lead | Agents can pay within limits without per-invoice approval; runway impact visible before authority is granted |
| Controller / audit | Every payment linked to clause, approval and mandate |
| Engineering / AI lead | Clear, programmatic spending boundary for agents |
| Security | AI has no keys; limit enforcement independent of the agent |

## Possible business models (to test, not commitments)
1. Per-mandate or per-agent platform subscription.
2. Usage-based fee on mandated payment volume.
3. Enterprise tier: approvals workflows, integrations, support.

## Competitive context (categories, not claims of superiority)
- Corporate cards and spend-management tools: strong controls for humans, limited machine-native contract linkage.
- Contract lifecycle management: extraction and tracking, no payment enforcement.
- Agent wallets and smart-account policy modules: enforcement, little contract semantics or accounting.
FluxMandate's differentiation is the *chain from clause to enforced limit to reconciled payment*. Whether that is defensible versus incumbents adding AI is an open question.

## Market size
No figure is asserted. Sizing requires validated counts of companies running payment-capable agents and their spend; this is a post-hackathon research item.

## Risks to commercial viability
- Agent-payment volume may grow slower than expected.
- Buyers may prefer fiat rails and incumbent cards.
- Regulatory treatment of automated payment authority varies by jurisdiction.
- Extraction accuracy on messy real contracts must be demonstrated.
