# Commercial thesis

FluxMandate is the control layer between signed commercial agreements and autonomous payment software. It complements rather than replaces ERPs, banks, contract-management products, wallets and accounting systems.

## Beachhead hypothesis

The first plausible customers are technology companies and crypto-native organizations whose agents purchase compute, data, APIs or SaaS under vendor agreements. This is a hypothesis, not validated demand.

| Buyer | Potential value |
| --- | --- |
| CFO / finance lead | Delegate routine payments without granting unlimited access |
| Controller / audit | Link a payment to its clause, approval and limit |
| AI engineering lead | Give agents a programmatic spending boundary |
| Security | Keep signing authority outside the model and bound maximum exposure |

## Competitive categories

- Spend-management tools control human cards but rarely start from contract semantics.
- Contract-lifecycle tools extract and track terms but do not enforce machine payments.
- Agent wallets enforce generic policies but typically lack contract evidence and company-impact simulation.

FluxMandate's proposed wedge is the full chain from clause to approved obligation to bounded authority to reconciled proof. Whether that remains defensible if incumbents add similar capabilities requires customer discovery.

## Possible models to test

1. Subscription per governed agent or mandate portfolio.
2. Usage pricing based on verified, mandated payment volume.
3. Enterprise workflow, integrations and audit exports.

No market-size, revenue, customer or extraction-accuracy claim is made in this prototype.
